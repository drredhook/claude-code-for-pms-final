Rook Dispatch · Final report · Claude Code for PMs

Release 4.2 didn't have a quiet August.
It taught four responders we'd stopped asking.

On 12 August we cut the ping wait from 90s to 60s and shifted routing weight toward proximity. Misses and tickets stepped up the same day. The numbers recovered on paper, but mostly because four responders were being pinged almost never. This report shows how I found that, what I propose, and how a reusable check keeps the next brief from repeating it.

What happened

In plain words

Before 12 Aug
Healthy. Acceptance 75–78% for six weeks. Missed pings ~2%. All 16 responders at 69–85%; the four who later vanished were not chronic decliners (69–81%, 0–4% missed).
12 Aug · release 4.2
Two changes ship together: ping wait 90s → 60s, and routing weights shift (proximity 0.45 → 0.60, recent acceptance 0.40 → 0.25).
12–14 Aug
Misses begin at once: 14 of 21 pings missed, against 1 of 51 on 5–11 Aug.
From ~18 Aug
Ping volume to four responders collapses. A missed ping costs the same −0.12 as a turn-down; only a taken ping gives +0.08 back; you can't take what you aren't offered.
Late Aug – Sept
Aggregate acceptance "recovers" (46.8% → 72.7%) because the four are barely pinged. Tickets keep rising (32 in the week of 24 Aug).
The previous call was "seasonal dip, don't revert." The data doesn't support it: acceptance, misses and tickets all step on the release day, turn-downs stay flat. Prior-year seasonality is untested, so the claim is "not supported", not "disproved".

Why it happened · leading explanation, not proven

A loop with no way out

Read from history.py, offer.py, config.py. A score only changes when someone is pinged. Nothing decays; nobody is removed; the 2019 TODO asks whether scores should recover and no one answered.

The code, in three lines

Taken
+0.08
Turned down
−0.12
Missed
−0.12

One identical call, once per ping. Break-even acceptance ≈ 60%. Scores floor at 0.

What it does and doesn't say

  • Fits the data: misses start immediately, volume falls after.
  • Cuts against "loop alone": 4.2 also lowered the acceptance weight, so the proximity change is an untested alternative.
  • Scores aren't in the database; confirming needs Wen Li.

02 · Super Hearing listen to users

Prompt I wrote (#8)
Do the call tickets tell a different story than the interviews?
Finding
Same story from different seats. Vanished pings (15 tickets) are the common, milder effect; quiet phones (30 tickets, none before 12 Aug) are the rare, severe one. Tickets rose about 4× after release, from ~6 to 28 a week. Caveat: Vesper and Meteor Mite have no tickets, and some ticket text repeats across responders.

03 · Rewind look at the data over time

Prompt I wrote (#8)
Run the other 12 responders by week
Finding
The "recovery" (46.8% → 72.7%) is mostly the four being pinged less (31, 16, 6, 3 a week), not people improving. The other 12 still miss ~19 pings a week, up from 1–3. An average hid four people. (I'd asked in #18 what I'd have missed with only the number, and this is the answer.)

04 · X-Ray Vision read the code

Prompt I wrote (#15)
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.
Finding
A miss and a turn-down are one identical −0.12 call. Exactly one thing ever raises a score: record_accepted, +0.08. No decay, no recovery, no removal. A responder who is never offered a ping can never earn a way back. Before 12 Aug the four were not chronic decliners, so the change hit everyone equally; whether that was a decision is still Wen Li's to say.

The fix · from the product brief and prototype

Restore their standing without reverting the timeout

Engineering could quietly revert 60s → 90s. Helen asked for something that still works for the responder with the faster window.

Release A · scoring

  • Miss and turn-down scored apart; a miss about −0.04
  • Scores ease back toward neutral; floor 0.2
  • Ping countdown on the phone
  • "Missed pings" list the responder opens herself or himself, with review requests
  • Handler "mark legit": a full, audited reversal
  • Misses with no opened event auto-reverse, unless over X% of that responder's pings (X a runtime setting, placeholder 25%)

Releases B and C

  • B: +15s grace once a ping is opened, "Your standing" card, support-queue routing, weekly report by responder
  • C: quiet-responder alert, shadow scoring, re-entry ping
  • Unchanged: the 60s wait. Release numbers are left to Helen and Marcus.
Honest limit: the grace only helps someone who has already opened the ping. A responder still climbing the stairs gets help from the lighter penalty and the review path, not the extra 15 seconds. Whether that is enough is open.

Keeping it from happening again

Before anyone acts on this

Still open

  • Cause isn't proven. Loop, 60s wait, or proximity weight: scores, display-vs-answer logs and travel times aren't in the database. Request drafted for Marcus and Wen Li, not sent.
  • Why the four started missing. Leading suspect: people who used to answer at 60–90s. Untestable without response times.
  • Scores live in memory. The fix assumes they persist; today a restart may reset everyone.
  • Prior-year seasonality is untested; Ravi's reports could settle it.
  • Roadmap: Committed vs quietly dropped (Availability Confidence) needs a conversation with Helen.
  • Data ends 7 Sep. Nothing after, and 83 of 107 post-release tickets are still open.
  • Security Policy 4.1 wasn't in the wiki; free-text risk is unsettled.
